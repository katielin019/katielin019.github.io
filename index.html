Hi, I'm Katie. I'm a software engineer and UX prototyper. My first job in the tech industry1 was doing product marketing at a small seed-stage startup. As with most startup gigs, I wore a lot of different hats. A few months in, I started taking on some small design tasks and found myself engrossed in the work. Not long after, I transitioned into a full-fledged product design role. One of the things I worked on was laying the foundation for a design system. Straddling the line between the design and engineering teams is kind of the sweet spot for me. All this means is that I'm just as comfortable creating components and variants in Figma as I am writing an API or developing a mobile app.

I just graduated 🎓 from Carnegie Mellon University with a B.S. in Information Systems.

What I'm proudest of from my time in college is the work I did as a teaching assistant. During the spring of my junior year, I worked as a teaching assistant for 67-272: Application Design and Development2. I enjoyed it a lot, and I felt like it suited my abilities and personality well. The following fall, I worked as a Head TA for 67-443: Mobile Application Development 3. In my final semester, I was the Head TA in charge of Curricular Development4 for 67-272.

COLOPHON

This website is a constant work in progress.

This iteration is written in pure HTML and CSS -- no frameworks, no static site generators, and no JavaScript6. It's set in the system default font stack used by your operating system.

It will continue to be hosted via GitHub Pages until I find the time to set up my own VPS.

1. My actual first job was working at a Kumon center when I was 16. I did the math program there for several years, so it was already a familiar place to me. I mostly graded worksheets and helped with math problems.

2. This is one of the core classes in the Information Systems department at Carnegie Mellon. It's designed to be framework-agnostic so that students learn the fundamentals of building (web-based) applications rather than learning to use whatever's trendiest at the moment. For the past several years, it's been taught using Ruby on Rails. In the spring of 2024, we added a new section about reactive programming using React on top of Rails to create dynamic interfaces.

3. 67-443 is a project-based course that's mostly focused on iOS development. In the past, an Android track was also offered, but the majority of students stick with Swift/SwiftUI (and before that, Objective-C and UIKit). When I took the class, no one chose to do Android development, but when I was a Head TA, there were a handful of students who opted for the Android track. It had been several years since enough students were interested in doing Android development, so some of the course materials were outdated. Because of my past Java experience and my fondness for the language, I functioned as the main "Android TA," updating labs and helping students debug in Android Studio. I rewrote the lab writeups and created new lab solutions that use Jetpack Compose, a declarative UI framework for Android that is analogous to Apple's SwiftUI. Students are also introduced to the Flutter framework, but don't use it extensively beyond one or two lab assignments and an in-class exercise.

4. I think this role and the experience I got from it are super unique, especially at the undergraduate level. My main responsibilities were to draft, write, and proof the exams we administered throughout the semester. Additionally, I also proofed and gave feedback on several lab write-ups and the specs for each phase of our semester-long project. With the continued rise of Generative AI, more weight and consideration is being given to written examinations than individual homework assignments or projects. Part of my job was to figure out how best to evaluate the students' growth vis-à-vis our learning objectives, and within the constraints of a written 80 minute exam. If you're curious about any of this and want to know more, send me an email. I've been interested in education and pedagogy for a while, and I'm always happy to chat about it.

5. This isn't a "now" page, per se, but it is a "now" section. If you have a personal website, you can make one too!

6. To be clear, I have nothing against any of these things. When I set out to create my personal website, I realized that the way I wanted it to look and function (for this iteration) didn't necessitate the use of a framework or SSG. I briefly considered using JavaScript to add light/dark mode toggling, which I may still do in the future, but I see no reason to do it now. Its exclusion doesn't take away anything content-wise from my website, so it's not a priority for me. As a developer, I am forever indebted to frameworks and open-source software. At the same time, I want to continue to be conscious of the way I wield my tools and the implications of the inclusion of any new technologies in any project because digital bloat is an environmental issue.
